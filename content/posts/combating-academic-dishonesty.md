When AI chatbots became popular, many schools turned to AI detection software to catch students who used them to write essays. These tools have a serious weakness. They guess whether text was written by AI, and their guesses are often wrong, sometimes flagging students who wrote every word themselves. Researchers have also found that detectors are more likely to wrongly flag writing by non-native English speakers. A false accusation of cheating can damage a student's trust in school for a long time.

AI Innovation Lab K-12 takes a different approach. Instead of guessing after the fact, it makes the learning process visible.

## Show the process, not just the product

When students write inside the program, it keeps a timeline of how the work was built: the outline, the first draft, feedback from the tutor, and each revision. Teachers can open the writing process view and see how long a student spent writing, how much they typed themselves, and whether any large blocks of text were pasted in.

A paste is not automatically a problem. Students paste in quotes they are going to cite, or their own notes from another document. The view simply gives the teacher the facts so they can have a fair conversation with the student if something looks off. The program never labels a student a cheater. The teacher makes that call.

## Make cheating less useful

The best way to reduce cheating is to design work where copying does not help much. That is why the tutor regularly asks students to explain their reasoning in their own words. A student who copied an answer from a chatbot has a much harder time explaining why it works. Short, frequent explanations also give teachers a sample of how each student writes, which makes it easier to notice when a final essay sounds nothing like them.

## Teach students how to use AI honestly

Banning AI completely is hard to enforce and leaves students unprepared for a world where they will use it constantly. A better approach is to teach students where the line is. In many classes, students can write their own class guidelines for when AI is a fair tool, such as brainstorming or checking grammar, and when it crosses the line, such as writing the essay itself. Students who help write the rules are more likely to follow them, and teachers can match those rules with the platform's [teacher controls](/educators/teacher-controls/).

## The tutor will not do the work

Finally, the program's own AI tutor is built so it cannot be used to cheat. It never writes a student's essay or gives a final answer to a problem. It asks questions, gives hints, and points out where an argument needs more evidence. Students who want a shortcut will not find one here, and students who want help will.
