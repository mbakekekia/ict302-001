"Adaptive learning" gets used a lot in education technology, and it does not always mean much. Here is what it means in this program, and why it matters at every grade level.

## The basic idea

In a traditional worksheet, every student gets the same twenty problems in the same order. Students who already understand the skill are bored by problem five. Students who are lost on problem two keep getting it wrong for eighteen more problems. Neither group learns much.

An adaptive program changes the next problem based on the last answer. Get a few right and the problems get harder. Make the same mistake twice and the program stops, figures out which step is causing trouble, and shows a worked example of just that step. The goal is to keep every student working at the edge of what they can do, where learning actually happens.

## What it looks like at each grade

In **kindergarten through second grade**, adaptive practice is short and playful. A student counting objects might get bigger numbers after a few right answers, or switch to counting with pictures if they are struggling. Sessions last about ten minutes, and the tutor speaks its hints aloud for early readers.

In **grades three through five**, students start working on longer problems, like multi-step fraction questions. The program watches each step, not just the final answer, so it can tell the difference between a student who does not understand fractions and one who made an arithmetic slip.

In **middle school**, the program starts asking students to explain their reasoning in writing. This is where the adaptive approach and academic honesty meet. A student who copied an answer cannot explain it, and the program notices.

In **high school**, students work on longer tasks such as research writing and multi-part algebra problems. Adaptive feedback focuses on planning, evidence, and revision, and students have more control over the pace and order of their work.

## What adaptive learning does not do

Adaptive learning does not replace the teacher. The program cannot notice that a student is having a hard week, and it does not know that the class needs a fire drill review on Friday. It handles practice so the teacher can handle people. Every recommendation it makes shows up on the teacher dashboard, and the teacher decides what to do with it.
