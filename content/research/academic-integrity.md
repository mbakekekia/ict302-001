When AI writing tools spread, many schools responded with detection software and stricter discipline. The research shows that this approach has a cost: it is damaging trust between students and teachers.

## The research

The Center for Democracy and Technology surveyed K-12 public school teachers about generative AI during the 2023-24 school year. It found that 68% of teachers regularly use AI detection tools, even though these tools are known to make mistakes. 64% said students at their school had gotten in trouble for using AI, or for being accused of using it, on an assignment, a 16 percentage point jump from the year before. And 52% of teachers agreed that generative AI has made them more distrustful of whether their students' work is really their own.

AI detectors guess whether text was written by AI, and their guesses can be wrong, flagging students who wrote every word themselves. A false accusation can do lasting harm to a student's relationship with school.

Source: [Center for Democracy and Technology, "Educators Still Struggling With Generative AI Detection, Discipline, and Distrust" (2024)](https://cdt.org/press/new-research-educators-still-struggling-with-generative-ai-detection-discipline-and-distrust-despite-increased-school-guidance/)

## What it means for AI Innovation Lab K-12

The platform does not try to catch students after the fact. It makes the learning process visible while it happens, so there is less to guess about.

{{< figure src="/images/program-integrity-report.svg" alt="Writing process view showing the timeline of an essay from outline to submission, 94 percent typed by the student, one large paste, and 74 minutes of writing time, with a teacher note that the paste was a cited quote." caption="Teachers see how work was built, not a guess about who wrote it." >}}

When students write or explain their thinking on the platform, teachers can see how the work was built: when it was started, how it was revised, and whether any large blocks of text were pasted in. A paste is not automatically a problem. Students paste quotes they plan to cite, or their own notes. The view simply gives teachers facts, so they can have a fair conversation if something looks off. The platform never labels a student as a cheater.

Just as important, the platform's own AI cannot be used to cheat. It does not write essays or hand over answers. And because students explain their reasoning often, in small pieces, teachers get to know how each student thinks and writes. That is the kind of relationship that rebuilds trust.
