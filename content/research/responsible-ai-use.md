Students will use AI for the rest of their lives, at work and at home. Schools have a chance to teach them to use it well: to know when it helps, when it is wrong, and when their own thinking has to come first.

## The research

A December 2025 College Board research brief by Adair, Congiusta, Howell, O'Grady, and Radford compared how high school students, parents, and teachers feel about generative AI. Teachers were the most concerned group. Nearly all of the AP teachers surveyed expressed worries about academic integrity and reduced deep learning, and more than 80% agreed that AI tools make students overly dependent on technology for basic tasks, hamper critical thinking, reduce original expression, and limit deep engagement with course material.

Those worries match the four problems AI Innovation Lab K-12 was designed to address: dependence on AI, the loss of originality and creativity, difficulty telling whether AI-generated information is reliable, and declining trust between students and teachers.

Source: [Adair, A., Congiusta, F., Howell, J., O'Grady, C., and Radford, A. W. (2025). Variation in High School Student, Parent, and Teacher Attitudes Toward the Use of Generative Artificial Intelligence. College Board Research](https://research.collegeboard.org/media/pdf/AI%20Research%20Brief%202_vf.pdf)

## What it means for AI Innovation Lab K-12

Responsible AI use is not just a rule students follow. It is a set of habits they practice. The platform builds three of them into everyday work.

**Think first.** In the [Project Testing Lab](/how-it-works/project-testing-lab/), every project starts with the student's own question and prediction. The AI helps them test the idea but never suggests it, which protects originality.

**Question the answer.** The [Evidence and Explanation Coach](/how-it-works/evidence-coach/) treats AI-generated text like any other unverified source. Students have to find where the information came from and whether it holds up, which builds the skill of judging AI output.

**Know when AI is off.** Teachers [decide when AI help is available](/educators/teacher-controls/), and students learn that some work, like tests and first drafts, is meant to show what they can do on their own.

The platform also keeps students' data private and never uses their work to train AI models. You can read more about that on the [privacy page](/educators/privacy/).
