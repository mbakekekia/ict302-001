When schools debate whether to allow AI, they are often debating something that has already happened. Students are using it at home, on their phones, and in study halls, with or without a school policy.

## The research

In 2025, the College Board surveyed high school students several times over the school year about how they use generative AI. As Howell and Romee (2025) reported, the share of high school students using generative AI tools for schoolwork rose from 79% in January 2025 to 84% by May. About half of students said they use AI to brainstorm, to revise or edit essays, and to research or find sources. ChatGPT was the most common tool.

The same research found that schools are split on what to do. About two in five schools or districts do not allow student use at all, while roughly one in five allow it without any formal policy. Principals and teachers named academic integrity and the loss of essential learning skills as their biggest worries.

Source: [College Board, "New Research: Majority of High School Students Use Generative AI for Schoolwork" (2025)](https://newsroom.collegeboard.org/new-research-majority-high-school-students-use-generative-ai-schoolwork)

## What it means for AI Innovation Lab K-12

If 84% of students are already using AI, banning it mostly means they use it without guidance. AI Innovation Lab K-12 takes the other path: give students an AI tool built for school, with teacher controls, and teach them to use it well.

That is why every tool on the platform keeps the student doing the thinking. [AI Learning Games](/how-it-works/learning-games/) gives hints instead of answers. The [Project Testing Lab](/how-it-works/project-testing-lab/) starts from the student's own idea. The [Evidence and Explanation Coach](/how-it-works/evidence-coach/) teaches students to question what AI tells them. Students still get the help they are looking for, but in a form that builds skills instead of replacing them.
