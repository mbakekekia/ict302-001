Teachers are in charge of every AI feature on the platform. Nothing reaches students that the teacher cannot see, change, or turn off.

## Set difficulty levels

Teachers set a difficulty range for each class, and for individual students when needed. The games adapt within that range, so a student who is far ahead does not jump three grade levels without the teacher knowing, and a student who is struggling does not drop below the level the teacher wants them working on. Teachers can lock a level for a test review day.

## Review AI-generated hints

Every hint the AI writes follows the same rule: guide, do not answer. Teachers can still review them. The hint review list shows the hints given in their class, and teachers can approve hints, edit them, or block ones that give too much away. Blocked patterns are used to improve future hints for that class. Teachers can also write their own hints for any question.

## Approve classroom resources and simulations

Students only see the sources, simulations, and games their teacher has approved. Teachers pick from the platform library, add their own trusted links, and turn items on or off for each unit. School librarians can build approved source lists that every teacher in the building can use.

## Determine when students can use AI

Teachers decide when AI help is on. They can turn AI off for quizzes and tests, allow it for practice and projects, and choose whether students can use it at home. A schedule setting can turn AI help off automatically during a test window. When AI is off, students can still use the platform for practice, but without hints or feedback.
