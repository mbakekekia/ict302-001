## What the feature does

The Project Testing Lab lets students test their own ideas with simulations. Instead of reading about how something works, students make a prediction, change the conditions, and see the result. The lab includes simulations for science (ecosystems, weather, forces and motion, energy), math (probability, data and statistics), and social studies (budgets, population, supply and demand).

## How students develop their own ideas

Every project starts with the student, not the AI. Students write a question they actually want to answer, such as "Would planting more trees make our playground cooler?" or "What happens to a town's budget if the bus fare goes up?" The AI helps them turn that question into a prediction they can test, by asking what they think will happen and why. It does not suggest the idea for them.

## How simulations test those ideas

Once students have a prediction, they choose a simulation and set it up. Each simulation has a few variables they can change, like the number of trees, the time of day, or the type of ground surface. Students change one thing at a time, run the simulation, and record what happens in a results table that builds automatically. This teaches the habit at the heart of science: test one change at a time and compare.

Teachers approve which simulations are available for each class, so students only see the ones that fit the unit.

## How AI helps students understand the results

When a simulation finishes, the AI does not tell students what the result means. It asks. If the data shows something surprising, like cooling that levels off after a certain number of trees, the AI asks the student what might cause that. Students write an explanation before they can run the next test. If an explanation does not match the data, the AI points to the part of the chart that disagrees and asks them to look again.

At the end of a project, students have a short report with their question, prediction, data, and explanation in their own words. Teachers can use it as is or have students present it to the class.
