## What the feature does

AI Learning Games turns skill practice into short, interactive challenges. Instead of a worksheet where every student gets the same twenty problems, each student gets a challenge that matches what they can do right now. Get a few right in a row and the game raises the level. Miss the same kind of problem twice and it slows down, switches to a simpler version, or shows a worked example of just the step that is causing trouble.

The games cover math and English language arts from kindergarten through high school, with a short science and social studies set for grades 3 to 8. Teachers choose which skills the games focus on, so practice always lines up with what is happening in class that week.

## How students interact with it

Students play on a Chromebook, laptop, or tablet, alone or in pairs. Each challenge takes one to three minutes. Students can answer by tapping, dragging, typing, or speaking, and younger students can have every question read aloud.

When a student is stuck, they can ask for a hint. The AI never gives the answer. The first hint is a question that points them in the right direction. The second shows a similar solved example. The third walks through the step they are on, and the student still has to finish it. Teachers can see which hints each student used, so they know who is working independently and who needs a check-in.

## Example activity: Fraction Quest

In a fourth grade unit on equivalent fractions, students play Fraction Quest. In one challenge, two friends are sharing pizzas cut into different numbers of slices, and the student has to make the shares equal. A student who picks the wrong answer gets a hint like "How much of the first pizza is shaded? Is that more or less than half?" A student who gets three right in a row moves to pizzas cut into sixths and eighths.

At the end of the game, each student writes one sentence explaining how they knew two fractions were equal. That sentence goes to the teacher, and it is often the clearest sign of who really understands the idea.
