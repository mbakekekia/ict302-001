The program ships with a full curriculum for math and English language arts, plus a short AI literacy unit for every grade band. Each unit is broken into small skills, and every skill has practice questions at several difficulty levels so the program can adjust to each student. Units are aligned to the Common Core State Standards, and districts can map them to their own state standards during setup.

## Grade bands

| Grade band | Math focus | ELA focus | AI literacy unit |
|---|---|---|---|
| K to 2 | Counting, place value, addition and subtraction | Phonics, sight words, short read-alouds | What is a computer helper? |
| 3 to 5 | Multiplication, fractions, measurement | Reading for main idea, paragraph writing | How apps learn from examples |
| 6 to 8 | Ratios, rates, equations, geometry | Argument writing, citing evidence | Spotting AI mistakes and bias |
| 9 to 12 | Algebra I and II, functions, statistics | Research writing, rhetoric, analysis | Using AI honestly in research |

## How a unit is built

A typical middle school math unit, such as Ratios and Rates, has about twelve skills and takes three to four weeks. Each skill has a short video or worked example, a practice set that adapts as the student works, and a two-question check that counts toward mastery. The unit closes with a performance task where students solve a real problem, like comparing phone plans or planning a class trip budget, and explain their reasoning in writing.

Writing units work the same way. Students plan, draft, and revise inside the program, and the tutor gives feedback on structure and evidence without rewriting their sentences for them. Teachers can see every stage of the draft in the [writing process view](/posts/combating-academic-dishonesty/).

## AI literacy in every grade

Students who use AI should understand it. Every grade band includes a short unit, about five lessons, on what AI is, where it gets things wrong, and when it is fair to use it. Younger students sort pictures to see how a computer learns from examples. High school students test a chatbot's answers against real sources and write their own class guidelines for AI use.
