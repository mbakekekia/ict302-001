A common worry about classroom technology is that every student ends up alone, staring at a screen. The program is designed to do the opposite. Most of the time it is a tool students use together, with the teacher moving around the room.

## Working in pairs

In pair mode, two students share a problem. One solves it and the other has to explain the answer back before they can move on. The tutor's question, usually some version of "why does this work?", becomes something the partners argue about out loud. That conversation is the point: saying an idea out loud to a classmate is one of the fastest ways to find out whether you really understand it.

## Small groups with the teacher

Because the dashboard groups students by the skill they are missing, the teacher can pull a few students to a table for a focused lesson. Everyone else keeps practicing on their own path, so no one is waiting. When the small group finishes, the program updates each student's path with what they just learned.

## Independent practice

Some days are for quiet practice. Students work through their own learning path and can request a hint at any step. Hints come in three levels: a nudge, a worked example of a similar problem, and finally a walkthrough of the step they are on. The program notes which level each student needed, so the teacher can see who is building independence and who is leaning on hints.

## Why it is set up this way

Students get to move at their own pace without raising their hand in front of the whole class every time they are stuck. The "explain it" questions can feel like extra work at first, but they build the habit of checking your own reasoning. Quieter students also get a low-pressure way to practice putting their thinking into words before sharing it with a group.
