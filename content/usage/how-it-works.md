The program is built to fit into a regular class period, not replace it. A teacher still plans the lesson and leads the room. The program handles the part that is hardest to do alone with 28 students: giving each one practice that matches what they actually know.

## Before class

Teachers pick a unit from the [curriculum](/usage/curriculum/) and choose which skills the class is working on that week. The program builds a short learning path for every student from those skills. There is nothing to grade ahead of time and no separate lesson plan to write.

## During class

Most teachers use a simple rhythm. The period opens with a five-minute warm-up that everyone does together on their devices. The program uses those answers to sort students quietly into what they need next. Students who are ready move on to new material, students who are close get targeted practice, and students who are stuck get a worked example before they try again.

While students work, the teacher watches the dashboard. It shows who is on track and who has missed the same kind of question more than once. It also suggests small groups, such as four students who all struggle with scaling a recipe, so the teacher can pull them to a table for a ten-minute mini-lesson while everyone else keeps going.

## The tutor asks why

When a student gets an answer right, the AI tutor often asks them to explain how they got it. When they get it wrong, it does not give the answer away. It asks a question that points them back to the step where things went off track. This keeps students thinking instead of guessing, and it gives the teacher a short written record of each student's reasoning.

## After class

Each period ends with a short exit ticket. The results roll into a weekly progress report that teachers can share with families or bring to a team meeting. Nothing has to be graded by hand, but teachers can override any score.

{{< callout title="What teachers keep control of" >}}
Teachers choose the skills, can turn the tutor off for a test, and can override any mastery score. The program makes suggestions. The teacher makes the decisions.
